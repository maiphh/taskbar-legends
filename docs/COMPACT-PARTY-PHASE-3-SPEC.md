Validation must assert definition keys cover all eighteen configured IDs exactly once, every ability ID resolves, every effect has a stack/expiry policy, and no new-family stat lookup changes legacy heroStats output. Data-only variants do not require eleven new bespoke resolver branches.

No healer's basic/active decision may stall out of range permanently. Movement and healing use the Phase 1 rules. Supports cannot cast on dead allies. Priority per ready action: emergency eligible defensive/heal active, other eligible ready active, then basic. A skipped ineligible active retains readiness; it does not consume its cooldown. Successful active cooldown begins at execution/release, not when merely considered. Priest's burst heal supplements the existing repeatable basic heal; do not replace all healing with a five-second cooldown. A unit starts only one action at a time, so active and basic healing cannot double-execute in one readiness decision.

## Deterministic ability and effect resolution

1. Keep all action timing on `combatTime(state)`, including CDs, statuses, projectile flight, assassin traversal, and guard readiness. Convert seconds to integer ticks at definition load.
2. Store active ability ID in the action state and resolve using a definition lookup, not a new hardcoded role switch in the renderer. The core remains image/DOM independent.
3. Effects store source ID, target ID, effect/group ID, magnitude, expiry tick, and stack policy. Same-group ordinary buffs/debuffs use the strongest magnitude; a reapplication refreshes expiry according to a named rule. Never multiply a buff repeatedly into an already-buffed base stat each tick.
4. Compute effective stats from base encounter stats plus currently active effects. Initial caps: total ordinary damage reduction <=50%; ordinary attack bonuses <=50%; attack-speed changes respect the existing minimum attack interval; DEF reduction <=60% with DEF >=0. Expiry removes contribution exactly once, including after snapshot/load.
5. Effects from a dead aura source cease at the death boundary. Timed already-applied effects can persist to expiry unless the definition explicitly says otherwise. A self-heal cannot resurrect its caster.
6. Protection/guard reservations occur before the damage batch. A guardian can intercept at most one hit per available cooldown; reserve immediately so two same-tick impacts cannot both consume the same ready intercept. Dead guardian, out-of-range ally, un-interceptable global AoE, and assassin traversal are ineligible. Once the assassin lands, its attack is interceptable. Revalidate redirected reach as an ability effect, not as a second ordinary melee swing.
7. Emit separate actual HP damage, shield absorption, healing, and effect/protection events or metrics. Do not count overkill as effective damage or overheal as effective healing. A death/reward is emitted once.
8. Proc counters advance only at the documented successful impact. Proc-triggered damage/healing cannot recursively trigger the same proc. Save counters, effects, and cooldowns explicitly; bounded history is not authoritative state.

Keep the Phase 1 mitigation contract for this slice: raw damage is `max(1, round(effectiveATK * abilityCoefficient * 100 / (100 + effectiveDEF) * (1 - cappedDamageReduction)))`, then absorb shields and clamp actual HP loss. Apply outgoing basic/passive multipliers once in the coefficient or effective ATK path, never both. Magic/projectile tags select behavior and presentation; they do not silently bypass DEF or add an unrequested resistance system. Healing uses `round(effectiveATK * healCoefficient)` clamped to actual missing HP; max-HP shield/heal percentages use the recipient's base encounter max HP. Splash coefficients describe a separate hit on eligible secondary targets; the primary must not receive both its main coefficient and an accidental duplicate secondary hit. Document any intentionally global boss action separately.

Use one common impact pipeline for arrived projectiles, due AoE and direct melee/casts. Capture eligible committed sources alive at phase start (already-released payloads are independently eligible after source death), validate targets, apply ready non-HP protection/status effects, and reserve intercepts. Calculate all damage payloads against that phase's effective defense before mutating any unit HP. Allocate shield absorption and actual HP damage per target in stable source/projectile order so overkill is never credited twice; commit the resulting batch and emit deaths/rewards once. Only then resolve heals on surviving casters/recipients and non-recursive success procs. A due shield protects regardless of source array order; a due heal cannot rescue a hero killed by that damage batch; mutual committed attacks remain possible. Aura loss takes effect at this death boundary, rather than changing damage calculation halfway through iterating units. A projectile must not bypass this pipeline and spend the same guard readiness separately from a direct impact in the same tick.

Initial shields share one strongest/refresh pool: retain the larger of remaining valid shield and new shield, and the later valid expiry. A weaker short shield cannot shorten or reduce a stronger existing pool. Save the protecting source identity of the retained pool; absorption events identify attacker, recipient and known protector separately so damage prevented is attributed correctly. An older fixture without protector metadata remains explicitly unattributed. Aura refreshes must not emit identical status events every tick; activation/source/magnitude changes are sufficient for presentation, and authoritative lifetime remains independent of event history.

## Ranged projectiles and AoE

A projectile releases after a valid in-range windup. Store stable projectile ID, source, target, world position, speed, snapshotted damage/effect payload, and lifecycle state. Initial speed is 500 world units/s, with at least one native tick in flight. It homes toward the living target and hits at body contact; a target leaving cast range after release does not invalidate the projectile. A dead/missing target expires the projectile without a hit or retarget. Source death does not erase an already released ordinary projectile. Snapshot/load continues the same flight once.

Snapshot the source's offensive ATK/coefficient and effect payload at release; apply the recipient's current effective DEF, damage reduction, shields and HP at impact. A defensive response after release must still protect against the incoming projectile. Do not precompute final mitigated HP damage at release. A success-count passive is awarded at the actual successful basic impact in stable projectile order; misses/expired targets do not consume its count. Keep the saved source unit's proc state available during encounter wind-down even after source death.

Update the real game's wave-completion check as part of this change. Otherwise the existing `all enemies dead -> win/rest` path stops combat ticks and silently discards the final enemy's already-fired projectile:

- If all deployed heroes are dead, declare loss and cancel remaining encounter effects. Do not grant post-defeat clear rewards from a later friendly projectile.
- If enemies are dead but living heroes remain and a released hostile projectile/AoE can still affect them, keep a bounded battle wind-down until that released impact resolves or expires. Dead sources do not start new actions. A hero killed by the last projectile can still cause a loss.
- With no actionable released hostile impacts, expire friendly projectiles aimed at dead targets and finish the wave/encounter normally. Never redirect old projectiles to a new wave's IDs.
- Give projectiles a serialized expiry deadline, initially 4 combat seconds after release, in addition to valid-target expiry; stage timeout remains authoritative. This prevents a missing/hard-to-reach entity from blocking a clear forever.
- Test this through `game.step()`, not only by calling the tick helper: last enemy fires, then dies before impact, and its projectile still lands while an ally survives. Test the last-hero-death result separately.

Render the projectile through the same world transform, including facing/arrow direction. A simple original canvas arrow/orb is sufficient; do not fetch or package reference-game art.

Extend compact camera framing for active threats. The Phase 1 camera considers only the party and nearest enemy; that is insufficient when a farther archer casts or an assassin crosses the formation. Build presentation bounds from living party bodies plus committed hostile windups, active marks/traversal, relevant projectile positions and telegraphed area extents. If these fit, use one integer translation with a small margin to frame them together. If they cannot all fit at the selected integer art zoom, retain the common world scale, prioritize the threatened ally/nearest pending hostile impact, and draw a restrained edge arrow/ability marker for each committed off-screen threat. Clamp only that UI marker, never the unit or projectile's world coordinate. Do not replace an attacking assassin with a `+N` count. Camera changes cannot mutate state, RNG or timing. Add a 480-wide browser case with separated committed threats proving each is either drawn in bounds or represented by its edge marker.

AoE records an explicit center, radius, eligible side, and impact tick. Snapshot the location at release, then include exactly living eligible bodies whose geometry intersects it at impact. Do not use every-party-member loops for a local explosion. Only an explicitly named global ability may hit the full party regardless of distance. A marked/telegraphed area uses the same center/radius as the damage query.

Distinguish a homing entity-target projectile from a ground-targeted AoE delivery. A wizard fireball locks the chosen world center at release; any delivery orb travels to that fixed center, and both its visible explosion and damage query use it. The original primary receives its main coefficient only if still eligible inside that area at impact; other eligible bodies receive the secondary coefficient once each. Primary death does not retarget the main coefficient to another body. A released ground AoE can still affect other eligible bodies after its original target/source dies, until its own deadline; an ordinary homing shot expires on target death. Bomber and boss marked areas follow the same fixed-center rule. Test a target moving after release so the visual and actual AoE cannot silently follow different locations.

## Party selection, equipment, and UI

Expose primary role plus strength/weakness and actual ability effects/cooldowns in Party. Show relevant timed status/mark indicators on the battlefield without continuous flashing. Explain an assassin loss differently from a timeout or frontline collapse.

Implement Balanced, Fast farm, and Boss sustain auto-party presets. Balanced chooses one tank, one DPS, one support when owned; use stable tie breaks and do not silently drop locked favorites. Other presets can choose nonstandard compositions. Healer is the onboarding-safe support, while a bruiser/debuffer or buffer team must demonstrate at least one useful matchup.

Auto-equip uses wearer-specific marginal utility. At minimum compare resulting damage throughput (including actual attack interval), effective HP/protection, and healing/buff contribution according to role. Never assign the strongest weapon to a tank just because it has the largest universal power score. Show numeric wearer stat deltas and explain the recommendation; final inventory lock/protection is Phase 4.

Implementation choices for this slice (do not leave these algorithms to UI guesswork):

- Extend `autoParty` with an optional validated `preset` of `balanced | fastFarm | bossSustain`; absence retains legacy behavior for legacy state, and means balanced for party-v2. Add optional `partyLocked` to owned heroes, default false, with an authority-validated toggle. A locked currently deployed hero keeps its current slot. A bench lock does not spontaneously deploy it. Manual party selection remains free; locking only constrains presets.
- Presets first select the remaining members, skipping already selected IDs. Balanced requests tank, DPS, support; prefer healer for its support request. Fast farm requests AoE DPS, single-target DPS, tank. Boss sustain requests guardian-preferred tank, single-target-preferred DPS, healer-preferred support. A locked deployed member satisfies its matching requested role/category before new members are selected; an already selected AoE DPS cannot also fill a second requested DPS member. If a category is absent, fall back through the remaining useful requested categories and then the strongest remaining owned hero. Never duplicate a hero or return fewer than three when three are owned. Surface missing role coverage instead of silently unlocking a favorite.
- Selection priority is not formation order. Preserve locked slots, then sort newly selected members by placement priority: tank, melee DPS, non-AoE ranged DPS, AoE DPS, support; assign them to the remaining slots from front to rear, with stable ID ties. Thus an unlocked Fast farm team still puts its tank in front, despite choosing the two DPS first. A deliberately locked rear tank remains there. Test both ordinary placement and retained locked positions.
- Within a requested tank category rank current derived effective HP `hp * (1 + def/100)` with a modest documented guardian-protection factor. Rank DPS by current `atk / attackTicks`, applying basic coefficient and a documented AoE preference only for fast-farm. Rank healers by actual healing coefficient / interval with active burst represented separately. Rank other supports by defined buff/debuff uptime. Resolve every exact tie by stable hero ID. Test low-rarity leveled/geared heroes against unbuilt high-rarity ones so rarity alone cannot win the selection.
- For auto-equip, compute actual candidate stats by temporarily assigning each eligible item in a clone, then use `heroStats`; never mutate live combat units or RNG while comparing. Use role-weighted, dimensionless utility relative to that same wearer's derived unequipped baseline: throughput `atk / attackTicks`, effective HP as above, and projected support/protection output from the definition's coefficients/cooldowns. Start weights DPS `(1,.25,.1)`, tank `(.2,1,.7)`, support `(.25,.5,1.2)` for throughput/EHP/support; document the exact support formula. This is an explainable heuristic, not a universal optimum.
- Candidate evaluation may clone only the wearer's equipment/owned-hero record while sharing read-only inventory/config references for `heroStats`. Do not repeatedly clone the whole simulation and event history for every item/wearer pair; comparisons must remain read-only and practical at inventory cap. Account for the previous wearer's stat loss as part of the same candidate move, including an equipped bench owner when applicable.
- For each gear slot, enumerate legal item/wearer pairs and their positive total-party utility gain, including the previous wearer's loss if moving an equipped item. Commit the greatest positive gain, recompute remaining pairs, and stop when no strictly positive gain remains. Deterministic pair tie break is hero ID then item ID. Do not cycle equal-score swaps, duplicate ownership, or assign an item by its current wearer's universal power rank. A bounded pass count is an additional safety guard; record if reached. Preserve bench-owned equipment unless its move has a positive party gain and remains legal. Item lock in Phase 4 prevents destruction; it need not forbid intentional equipment use.
- Party displays the current preset selector and per-member lock, plus a clear explanation of unfilled recommended coverage. Equipment comparison shows numeric HP/ATK/DEF/attack-interval changes and the role-weighted recommendation. Tests must demonstrate a DPS-favorable weapon goes to the DPS while tank-favorable armor goes to the tank in an explicit three-hero fixture.

In party throughput and healing projections, use the realized basic cycle `max(derived attackTicks, windupTicks + minimumRecoveryTicks)`. The action pipeline's lower bound is 6 ticks (4 windup + 2 recovery), while `heroStats` preserves the actual `CONFIG.runtime.minAttackIntervalTicks` value of **8** for both rulesets. Therefore the fastest normally derived basic cycle with current data is 8 ticks. The Party basic-rate display, comparison deltas, preset ranking and auto-equip scoring must agree. Test two extreme-speed loadouts whose pre-clamp intervals would be 4 and 2: both derive 8 and must show unchanged 8-tick throughput. A separate synthetic helper test may verify that custom intervals 4 and 2 both have the 6-tick action floor, but must not claim those are normally reachable gear stats. Preserve legacy calculations.
